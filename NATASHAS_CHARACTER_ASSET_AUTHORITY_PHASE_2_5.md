# NATASHAS — CHARACTER & ASSET AUTHORITY
## Phase 2.5 — Canonical Visual Content Standard

**Product:** NATASHAS — Naija Table Shakers  
**Tagline:** Power belongs to whoever can keep it.  
**Parent Ecosystem:** GameForge  
**Authority Level:** Canonical  
**Relationship:** Subordinate to the NATASHAS Master Game Constitution; implementation-aligned with the NATASHAS Master Blueprint.

---

## 1. Purpose

The NATASHAS Character & Asset Authority (NCAA) defines the canonical system for creating, selecting, validating, versioning, storing, delivering and using characters, environments, props, vehicles, wardrobe, animation, VFX, UI-facing art, audio and other game assets.

Its primary objective is to prevent visual inconsistency, regional inaccuracy, asset duplication, identity drift, performance failures and uncontrolled AI generation.

The governing principle is:

> **One canonical world, many contextual manifestations.**

A character may appear in 2D, 3D, portrait, social, cinematic and gameplay contexts, but should remain recognisably the same character.

---

## 2. Asset Authority Hierarchy

The order of authority is:

1. NATASHAS Master Game Constitution
2. NATASHAS Master Blueprint
3. NATASHAS Character & Asset Authority
4. Canonical Character/Asset Master
5. Approved variant
6. Runtime derivative
7. Temporary prototype/concept

A lower-level artifact may not contradict a higher-level authority.

---

## 3. Asset Lifecycle

Every important asset follows:

**REQUEST → BRIEF → GENERATE/PROCURE → REVIEW → REVISE → VALIDATE → LOCK → REGISTER → OPTIMISE → DEPLOY → MONITOR → VERSION**

Asset states:

- Concept
- Prototype
- Candidate
- Review
- Approved
- Locked
- Deprecated
- Retired

Only Approved/Locked assets may become canonical production content.

---

## 4. Character Classes

### Hero Characters
Recurring political, social, business, media and narrative actors.

Require full Character Bible, canonical identity and controlled variants.

### Supporting Characters
Important recurring actors with moderate identity requirements.

### NPC Archetypes
Population characters generated from controlled archetypes, regional context and gameplay role.

NPC generation must not rely on simplistic or offensive stereotypes.

---

- formal wardrobe

The Character Bible is the identity anchor for future generation.

---

## 7. 2D / 3D Identity Rule

2D and 3D representations of the same character must derive from one canonical identity definition.

The objective is not pixel-perfect replication across all rendering technologies; it is recognisable identity consistency.

A character portrait must not depict one face while the 3D model depicts a materially different person.

## 9. Regional Identity Engine

NATASHAS must never portray Nigeria as one generic environment.

Each state and location receives a Regional Identity Profile.

The profile may affect:

### Visual DNA
Architecture, roads, signage, vegetation, public spaces, markets, building forms and street props.

### Transportation DNA
Vehicle mix, public transport presentation, terminals, traffic patterns and transport soundscape.

### Fashion DNA
Everyday clothing, professional clothing, traditional clothing, youth styles and context-appropriate wardrobe.

### Audio DNA
Ambient noise, transport sounds, markets, crowds, radio styles, language flavour and locally appropriate musical influences.

### Social DNA
NPC interaction patterns, commerce, greetings, public-space behaviour and local atmosphere.

### Environmental DNA
Terrain, vegetation, weather, lighting and time-of-day conditions.

### Economic DNA
Commercial and occupational environments that plausibly fit the location.

Regional identity must be authentic without reducing a place to caricature.

---

## 10. Regional Transition

Travelling between locations should gradually alter:

- scenery
- architecture
- road character
- vehicles
- clothing
- signs
- ambient sound
- music layers
- NPC population mix
- weather/environment

The player should be able to feel that they have moved to another part of Nigeria.

---

## 11. Context-Driven Asset Selection

Assets should be selected by context rather than by arbitrary visual choice.

Every generation/request should provide:

**WHO**  
**WHERE**  
**WHEN**  
**WHAT**  
**WHY**  
**MOOD**  
**ROLE**  
**REGION**  
**WEATHER**  
**CULTURAL CONTEXT**  
**GAMEPLAY PURPOSE**  
**PERFORMANCE TARGET**

Example:

> Governor + Kwara + urban district + daytime + infrastructure inspection + hot weather + official visit + mobile performance tier.

The system then determines the relevant character, wardrobe, vehicle, NPC, environment and sound assets.

---

## 12. Asset Variants

Prefer controlled variants over unrelated duplicate assets.

Examples:

Character → wardrobe variants → animation variants → environment variants → lighting variants.

Vehicle → colour/brand-neutral variants → damage states → weather states → performance tiers.

Environment → day/night/rain/dry → low/medium/high detail.

---

## 13. Asset Performance Tiers

Assets must be delivered in controlled performance classes:

- Low
- Mobile
- Medium
- High
- Showcase

The runtime selects assets according to device capabilities, network conditions, scene importance and user settings.

---

## 14. Prototype vs Production Asset

A browser-generated visual showcase is not automatically a production asset.

Production validation may require:

- correct file format
- topology/geometry quality
- rigging
- animation clips
- texture optimisation
- compression
- texture atlases
- LODs
- collision representation
- draw-call considerations
- memory budget
- loading budget
- licensing clearance
- runtime validation

---

## 15. Audio Authority

Audio follows the same canonical identity rules as visuals.

Audio packs may include:

- ambience
- transport
- market
- crowd
- government/public spaces
- weather
- UI feedback
- event stingers
- music layers
- voice profiles

Audio must be contextual, regional and appropriately licensed or original.

---

## 16. Cultural Quality Control

Regional assets should be reviewed for authenticity and respectful representation.

Where appropriate, Nigerian creators, cultural reviewers or subject-matter reviewers should be included in validation.

Avoid flattening regional identities into stereotypes.

---

## 17. Asset Registry

GameForge should maintain an authoritative registry containing:

- metadata
- ownership/origin
- licensing
- compatibility
- dependencies
- performance
- previews
- versions
- usage history
- generation lineage
- approval state

Every runtime reference should resolve to a known asset identity.

---

## 18. Asset Lineage

AI-generated or transformed assets should retain lineage:

- source prompt/brief
- source asset(s)
- generation system
- modification steps
- reviewer
- approval date
- version

This allows reproduction, auditing and replacement.

---

## 19. Asset Consistency Tests

Before locking an asset, validate:

### Identity
Does it match the canonical character/asset?

### Context
Does it belong in the intended location and scenario?

### Style
Does it belong to the NATASHAS visual universe?

### Culture
Does it reflect the intended Nigerian context respectfully?

### Performance
Can target devices render it?

### UX
Does it improve gameplay rather than clutter it?

### Licensing
Can it legally be used and distributed?

---

## 20. Character/Asset Generation Responsibility

### Claude Design
Visual exploration, prototyping, 2D/3D concept generation, art-direction exploration and iterative visual refinement.

### GameForge
Asset registry, metadata, lineage, storage, versioning, compatibility, generation workflows, marketplace integration and distribution infrastructure.

### Claude Code / Engineering Agents
Runtime integration, loading, state mapping, animation control, gameplay binding, optimisation and testing.

### Human Review
Final canonical approval for hero characters, culturally sensitive content, major regional assets, licensing and production-critical content.

---

## 21. Regional Production Strategy

Do not generate all 36 state asset libraries blindly at the start.

Validate the Regional Identity Engine using contrasting regions first.

Recommended prototype set:

- Kwara
- Enugu
- Lagos

Optionally add:

- Kano
- Rivers

Once the regional methodology is validated, scale systematically across all states.

---

## 22. Signature NATASHAS Test

A successful regional implementation should pass this test:

> **“This feels like where I am.”**

not merely:

> “This says where I am.”

---

## 23. Canonical Character/Asset Rule

No developer, AI agent, designer or content generator should invent a new version of an existing canonical identity without checking the Asset Authority first.

The system must prefer:

**REUSE → VARIANT → DERIVATIVE → NEW ASSET**

in that order.

---

## 24. Relationship to GameForge

NATASHAS is the first flagship use case for the GameForge asset architecture.

The asset system should be designed so that lessons from NATASHAS become reusable GameForge infrastructure for future GameForge games.

---

# FINAL AUTHORITY PRINCIPLE

> **Every character, environment, prop, vehicle, wardrobe item, animation, visual effect and audio element must have a reason to exist, a known identity, a defined context, a controlled lineage and a validated path into the live game.**
